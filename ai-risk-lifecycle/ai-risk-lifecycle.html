Regulatory & standards implications — CancerDetect

EU AI Act High-risk

Used as a medical device, CancerDetect is high-risk, triggering the full regime:

  • Art. 9 risk-management system — continuous & iterative (this whole lifecycle)
  • Art. 10 data governance & bias control
  • Art. 11–12 technical documentation & automatic logging
  • Art. 13 transparency / instructions to clinicians
  • Art. 14 human oversight (the human-in-loop control)
  • Art. 15 accuracy, robustness & cybersecurity

Conformity assessment + CE marking, and post-market monitoring (the drift row).

OECD AI Principles Principles

Five values-based principles that underpin the EU AI Act and most national AI policy:

  • Inclusive growth, well-being
  • Human-centred values & fairness → bias, societal rows
  • Transparency & explainability → XAI row
  • Robustness, security & safety → safety, infosec, reliability
  • Accountability → accountability row

Not law, but the shared vocabulary regulators build on.

ISO/IEC 42001 Certifiable AIMS

The management-system wrapper — how you run all of the above, repeatably (Plan-Do-Check-Act):

  • Cl. 6.1 AI risk assessment & treatment (this navigator is that process)
  • AI system impact assessment (Annex A) → the Map / impact rows
  • Annex A controls — lifecycle, data, third-party, transparency
  • Cl. 9–10 performance evaluation & continual improvement → monitoring

Certification demonstrates governance to regulators and clients; complements, not replaces, NIST AI RMF.

Turning this into an application

This is the data model for a real tool. Each risk is an object with its NIST references, inherent and residual ratings, trustworthiness trait, ethics lens, and a control per lifecycle stage. A working app would let an assessor add a system, select applicable risks, record evidence and ratings, attach controls, and export a risk register — exactly the finding format already built into the LLM Top 10 Lab. It slots directly beside the security-assessment-framework and the GRC risk-consolidator toolkit.

Worked example for education and framework teaching. Framework references (NIST AI RMF 1.0, NIST SP 800-37 Rev 2 / 800-30, ISO 31000, ISO/IEC 27005, ISO/IEC 42001) and EU AI Act article numbers are paraphrased and mapped at a conceptual level — verify exact clause and article numbers against the source documents before formal use. Trustworthiness characteristics follow NIST AI RMF: valid & reliable, safe, secure & resilient, accountable & transparent, explainable & interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful bias managed.
